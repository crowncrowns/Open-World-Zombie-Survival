## Conclusion

The existing shell is recognizable as the intended blue shopping centre, and the market/bed/cable/sign vocabulary is present. The current delivered images still look like a procedural early game environment rather than the requested realistic inhabited hub. More bevels or larger texture dimensions alone will not close the gap. Main deficiencies are functional depth, coherent construction of close objects, deliberate asymmetry, material hierarchy, and the overhead architecture/light relationship.

Two floors, 93 x 130 m outer walls, the accepted rounded front, and the explicit removal of the entrance canopy remain valid user requirements. The concept interiors depict additional galleries; do not add a third floor just to match those pictures. Right and hidden sides have no exact reference. No original concept establishes the full internal floor plan, so invented functional rooms must be identified as reasoned additions.

## Eight highest-impact corrections

1. **P0, atrium roof and daylight.** In `market_reference` and `gallery_reference` the overhead plane occupies roughly half the composition and reads as streaked opaque grey sheet with very thin flat rails. The references show clear daylight glazing, substantial white triangular roof trusses and glazing subdivisions. From outside (`top45`) the current glass is visibly transparent, so the interior appearance should not be diagnosed simply as an opaque shader without checking faces, environment, normals, glass tint and render state. Add properly supported structural truss depth and connection plates; preserve walk surfaces and roof route; reduce uniformly dirty grey surface appearance and verify inside/outside under the same daylight. This changes the principal architectural impression, not just a decorative detail.

2. **P0, use the depth of the building.** `extra_alley`, `gallery_reference`, front glazing and the rear end of the market expose long empty uninterrupted floor/ceiling strips behind the inhabited front row. Repeated wire enclosures and identical bunk bays do not establish functional rooms. Plan recognizable kitchen/dining, medical, repair, stores, utilities/water, guard and private family areas in the existing shell. Give each a complete working sequence (storage > work surface > output/waste/clean-up), partitions, access and service connections. Reserve intentional clear routes, not generic empty lanes. Do not fill every gap with random debris or fabricate unseen reference measurements.

3. **P0, replace visibly primitive close silhouettes.** In `detail_stall`/`material_sunny` a kettle-like vessel is effectively a square block and bent strip; bottles, bowls, sacks and produce show coarse facets; tools are poorly differentiated straight bars, barrels/containers lack convincing formed parts. In `floor_grazing` sandbags read as identical flattened octagonal pellets with a black rectangle. Rebuild the *visible hero sets* from real construction: spun kettle/pot body, rim/handle/lid/spout; bottle neck and bottom; crimped cans with labels; socket/wrench jaws; stitched compressed sacks; folded fabric with thickness/contact. Increase segments where the silhouette needs them, not everywhere. Preserve reusable roots and IDs.

4. **P0, market and living layout are too repetitive.** `market_reference` has a symmetrical large corridor flanked by straight rows of the same stall frame/height/sign/cargo combination. The reference composition relies on dense overlapping activities, uneven shop widths and depths, interruptions, cross connections and active central gathering. In `extra_in_scene_gallery`, identical bunk, screen, plant, rug units repeat in a line. Design at least three materially/constructionally distinct stall types and family/work bays; vary *function and composition*, not only position by a few centimetres. Offset counter fronts, add side awnings/shelves/infill and deeper back-room functions, maintain checked movement envelopes. The central hearth needs a recognizable social arrangement with purposeful seats, cups/food, fuel and safe clear paths, not four isolated chairs around an emissive barrel.

5. **P1, signs, tarps and personal graphics.** Current signs dominate the same position on almost every frame, use oversized bright pristine letters and repeat categories indiscriminately. Some letters have dark discontinuities/cuts that read as geometry artifacts rather than faded printing (checkpoint header/rules). The current graphic logo/letter shape is substantially simplified relative to the reference. Rail cloth has large regular smooth folds and black rectangular areas; the reference uses assorted weight, material, attachment and ragged edges. Use separate reusable graphic/cloth variants with correct print hierarchy, margins, muted paint, aged alpha and local stitching/eyelets/ties. Make damage small and directed, keep words legible at player distance, and inspect close contact so paint does not float or intersect.

6. **P1, material ageing needs causes and scale.** `floor_grazing` shows broad smooth cloud/marble-like stains covering nearly every tile but straight immaculate uninterrupted grout. Reference dirt collects in tracks, around stalls, joints, thresholds and contact zones; broken/changed tiles and isolated damp areas create variation. Keep the white/red ceramic palette and physical 0.5 m scale; add restrained directional traffic masks, local grout grime, repaired/broken patches and drying edges, with clean paths as a different state. Cladding close-up shows the same elongated noise/streak aesthetic that appears on other unrelated materials. Wood still reads smooth flat brown from the normal play camera, with weak end grain and insufficient exposed layered construction. Evaluate texture appearance at player distance, not family/map count.

7. **P1, lighting is repeated glowing balls, not believable services.** The visible low-sided bulbs appear as uniformly saturated flat pale polygons; repeated bright rows give every banner identical yellow light while the large soffits/backgrounds become flat dark planes. References mix shielded work lights, dangling practicals, reflected warm light and cool roof illumination, with wires, junctions and improvised routing supporting each source. Give sockets/cages/shades real shapes, reduce overlarge apparent bulbs and fit emissions to a lamp type, break up cable circuits and lamp intervals, light complete inhabited bays rather than banners alone. Need actual daylight/night and material close-ups; do not infer a final baked/GI or FPS result from these images.

8. **P1, exterior construction/history layer is still sparse.** Rounded silhouette and blue cladding are recognizable, but current front ground floor is almost continuous full glazing; reference lower floor has stronger cream solid masses/shop bays around a concentrated entrance. Any alteration should preserve the accepted stage 3 direction and frame a clearly justified local correction. `top45` shows a largely blank roof with sparse tiny boxes/cabins versus layered watch positions, sandbag parapets, tarp repairs and communication/service equipment in the references. Left and rear are long repeated blue rectangles without convincing occupation/service clusters. Add a *few complete* material-reuse interventions with bracing, access, cabling, stored supplies and repair seams rather than more identical tiny props. Freight opening remains deferred: enrich a closed service/workshop yard without cutting a new cargo portal. City, vehicles and NPC crowds are context, not a requirement to import wholesale or evidence that the shell itself is accurate.

- Fixed-camera matched before/after for front, market, checkpoint, gallery and a real previously empty deep bay; lighting changes separately labelled.
